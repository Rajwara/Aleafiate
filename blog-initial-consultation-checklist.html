Preparing for your initial consultation: a simple checklist

A little preparation helps you and your practitioner make the most of your first appointment. Use this checklist in the days before you meet.

1. Start with a free discovery call

If you haven’t already, book a free, no-obligation call with our team. We’ll talk through your health needs, explain how everything works and let you know whether our service may be right for you — before you commit to anything.

2. Complete your intake form early

Your intake form gives your practitioner your medical history and current health information ahead of time. Completing it a day or two before your appointment means your consultation can focus on you, rather than paperwork.

3. Gather your health information

  • A list of all medicines and supplements you take, with doses if you know them.
  • Any diagnoses, recent test results or letters from specialists.
  • A note of treatments you’ve already tried, and how they worked for you.
  • Your GP’s details, if you’d like us to be able to contact them with your consent.

Why medicines matter: we use eScript software to check for conflicts with prescriptions from other practitioners. Telling us about everything you take helps keep your care safe.

4. Think about your goals

What would you most like help with? How do your symptoms affect your sleep, work or mood? Jotting down a few notes helps you explain your situation clearly — and makes sure the plan fits your life.

5. Prepare your questions

There are no silly questions. You might want to ask how long a plan usually runs, how follow-ups work, or what costs to expect. Our FAQ page answers many common questions too.

6. Know the practical details

  • Initial consultations are by telehealth or in clinic at Logan Central Plaza, 38/74 Wembley Rd.
  • Card details are pre-authorised before your consultation, and payment is processed afterwards.
  • If you need to reschedule, please give us at least 24 hours’ notice.

This article is general information only and isn’t medical advice. For advice about your own health, please speak with a registered healthcare professional.
